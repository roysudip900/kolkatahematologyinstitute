> Lymphoma is a blood cancer of the lymphocytes, the white cells that live in the glands (lymph nodes), and its main sign is a painless lump in the neck, armpit or groin. Many types can be treated and some removed completely, though lymphoma can come back. A lump that stays or grows, especially with fever, night sweats or weight loss, needs a doctor.

The lump in her neck did not hurt, so she left it alone.

She is twenty-two, in her final year at a college in Kolkata, living in a hostel. She first felt it in September, a smooth swelling on the side of her neck, and thought it came from the sore throat she had had. By November it was bigger. Her skin itched at night, she woke up with her pillow damp with sweat, and she was too tired to finish her notes. Her exams start in February.

In a village outside Bardhaman, a farmer of sixty has lost so much weight that his kurta hangs loose. He has had a fever on and off for weeks, and there is a lump in his armpit that he noticed while bathing. His son has brought him to the city.

She wants to know whether she can sit her exams. His son wants to know whether his father can be treated at his age.

# What lymphoma is

Lymphocytes are white blood cells that fight infection. They travel through thin tubes and small glands called lymph nodes, found all over the body, most easily felt in the neck, armpits and groin. In lymphoma, lymphocytes become cancerous and build up, most often in the lymph nodes, which swell into painless lumps.

There are two main types. Hodgkin lymphoma is less common and mainly affects young people and older people. Non-Hodgkin lymphoma is a large group of more than sixty types, some slow-growing and some fast-growing, and it mostly affects older people.

# Why her lump needed a doctor

Glands often swell with a sore throat or other infection, and these usually go down within a week or two. A gland that does not go down, keeps growing, feels hard or does not move needs a doctor's look, especially with night sweats, itching or fever.

The main test for lymphoma is a biopsy, in which a small piece of the swollen lymph node, or the whole node, is taken out and examined. The type of lymphoma decides the treatment.

# Why his fever and weight loss mattered

Lymphoma can cause fevers that come and go, drenching sweats at night, weight loss without trying and itchy skin. On their own, these have many causes. Together with a lump that does not go away, they point towards the lymph glands.

# Signs of lymphoma

- a painless lump or swelling in the neck, armpit or groin, the most common sign
- fever, or feeling hot, cold or shivery
- drenching sweats at night
- weight loss without trying
- itchy skin
- breathlessness

If the blood report does not show a cause, see a hematologist if you have these signs.

A gland (lymph node) that has not gone down after two weeks, or keeps growing, needs a doctor, who may advise a biopsy. Left for months, if the cause is lymphoma, it can spread to other parts of the body and become harder to treat.

# What treatment can do

Treatment depends on the type of lymphoma, how fast it grows, where it is, whether it has spread, and the person's general health. It is planned by a team of specialists and may include:

- chemotherapy, sometimes with steroids taken only as the hematologist plans
- targeted medicines, which act on the lymphoma cells, and immunotherapy, which helps the body's defences attack the lymphoma
- radiotherapy to the affected glands
- a stem cell transplant for some people whose lymphoma comes back

In Hodgkin lymphoma, treatment can often remove the lymphoma completely, especially if it is caught early. It can come back, and then it can often still be treated. Fast-growing non-Hodgkin lymphoma needs treatment straight away, and treatment can often remove it completely, though sometimes more treatment is needed later. Slow-growing non-Hodgkin lymphoma usually cannot be removed, but treatment can keep it under control for many years.

Some treatments can affect the chance of having children later. Young people should ask about this before treatment starts.

# How the stories go on

The student's biopsy shows Hodgkin lymphoma. Before treatment starts, her hematologist talks with her about having children one day. Her college lets her write her exams later. Her hostel friends take turns going with her to the hospital.

The farmer's biopsy shows a fast-growing non-Hodgkin lymphoma. His age and general health are checked, and treatment begins in the city. His son learns which signs mean the hospital, and keeps the reports in one folder.

# Go to a hospital the same day if

- difficulty breathing or swallowing
- a face, neck or arms that suddenly swell, or swollen veins in the neck or chest
- a high fever with shivering, above all during treatment

# Take these questions to your doctor

A hematologist leads the care of lymphoma. Bring your reports and ask:

- Is a biopsy needed, and of which lump?
- Is this Hodgkin or non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and which type?
- Is it slow-growing or fast-growing, and has it spread?
- What will treatment aim for?
- Will treatment affect having children later?
- What should we do if a fever comes during treatment?

# Common questions

## Is a painless lump in the neck cancer?

Usually not. Glands often swell with infections and go down within a week or two. A lump that stays, grows, feels hard or comes with fever, night sweats or weight loss needs a doctor.

## Is lymphoma curable?

Many types can be treated, and some can be removed completely, such as Hodgkin lymphoma and fast-growing non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Even then it can come back, and sometimes more treatment is needed. Slow-growing types usually cannot be removed but can be kept under control for many years.

## What is the difference between Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma?

Both are cancers of the lymphocytes, but they are different illnesses and are treated differently. Hodgkin lymphoma is less common and mainly affects young people and older people.
