> A low white cell count means the body has fewer of the cells that fight infection, so infections can come more easily and become serious quickly. Causes include some medicines, cancer treatment, infections and illnesses of the bone marrow. Never stop a medicine on your own because of a report. If your count is low and you get a fever, go to a hospital at once.

# What white cells do

White cells protect the body from infection. The commonest kind, the neutrophil, is the body's first line of defence. Each neutrophil lives less than a day, so the bone marrow has to make new ones all the time. The other main kind, the lymphocyte, helps control the body's defences and makes antibodies.

A low white cell count usually means the body is not making enough white cells. When the neutrophils are low, it is called neutropenia. With fewer cells to fight germs, infections come more easily, and a simple infection can turn serious quickly.

# What can cause it

- cancer treatment, such as chemotherapy and radiotherapy
- some medicines, including some medicines for an overactive thyroid and some medicines for mental illness
- some cancers, such as leukaemia
- infections, such as HIV or hepatitis
- autoimmune conditions, such as rheumatoid arthritis

The doctor decides which tests are needed, from your history, your medicines and your report.

# Signs

A low count does not always cause symptoms. It is often found on a blood test done for another reason. The main sign is getting infections often, or an infection that is worse than usual:

- a fever, or feeling hot, cold or shivery
- a sore throat
- mouth sores that keep coming back
- toothache
- skin rashes
- tiredness or a flu-like illness

# What treatment can do

Treatment depends on the cause.

- An infection is treated quickly, often with antibiotics, sometimes in hospital.
- If a medicine or other treatment is the cause, the doctor may stop it or change how much is given. Do not stop or change any medicine on your own.
- Some people are given medicine to boost the white cells, or medicine to help prevent infection.
- During cancer treatment, the team checks the count with regular blood tests.
- The illness behind the low count, if there is one, is treated by the hematologist.

# Lowering the risk of infection

When your count is low:

- Wash your hands with soap and water often.
- Stay away from people who are ill.
- Store and cook food properly. Do not eat raw or undercooked meat, fish or eggs.
- Do not share food, cups, spoons, toothbrushes or make-up.
- Use an electric shaver rather than a razor.
- Do not walk barefoot outside, do not swim in ponds or rivers, and do not handle animal dung or change nappies.
- Before taking any medicine for a fever, even paracetamol or a painkiller, ask the doctor, because it can hide the signs of a more serious problem.

# Go to a hospital now if

Go at once, and do not drive yourself, if your white cell count is low, or you are on chemotherapy or a medicine known to lower it, and you have:

- a fever, or feeling hot, cold or shivery
- a sore throat with fever, or mouth sores that stop you swallowing
- difficulty breathing
- confusion or slurred speech
- blue, pale, grey or blotchy skin, lips or tongue
- passing very little urine

Tell them your count is low or that you are on chemotherapy.

# Take these questions to your doctor

- What is my white cell count, and which kind of white cell is low?
- What could be causing it, and which tests do I need?
- Could one of my medicines be the cause? Who will decide whether to change it?
- Do I need medicine to prevent infection?
- What should I do if I get a fever, and where should I go?
- When will the count be checked again?

# Common questions

## My count is low but I feel well. Is it serious?

Not always. A low count can be found on a test done for another reason. Show the report to a doctor, who will ask about your health and medicines and may repeat the test.

## I am on thyroid tablets and have a sore throat and fever. What should I do?

Go to a hospital at once and tell them about your tablets. Some thyroid medicines can lower the white cells. Do not stop the tablets on your own; the doctors decide.

## What can I eat when my white cell count is low?

Mostly yes, but avoid raw or undercooked meat, fish and eggs, store food properly, and do not share plates and cups. Ask the team if you need special advice.
